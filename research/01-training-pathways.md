# 01 — How EOD / Bomb Technicians Are Trained (International Pathways)

Research date: September 2026. Scope: organizational and educational structure of professional
training only. No device construction, render-safe procedures, formulations or tactics are
recorded here. Where a public source mentions procedures, only the *name of the topic area* is
kept (e.g. "fuzing theory"), never its content.

Verification key: **verified 2026-09** = fetched with WebFetch in Sept 2026 and content matched.
**could not verify** = automated fetch refused (HTTP 403/429/404); facts from that URL are
cross-checked against another verified source where possible.

## 1. United States

### 1.1 Military: Naval School Explosive Ordnance Disposal (NAVSCOLEOD), Eglin AFB, Florida
- Navy-led, jointly staffed (Army, Navy, Air Force, Marine Corps) school delivering basic and
  advanced EOD training to all four US services, partner nations and selected US civilians.
  Opened 1988. Parent command: Center for EOD & Diving. [S1, S2]
- Basic course is roughly 41–42 weeks (sources differ; figures change as the syllabus is
  revised). [S2, S3]
- Organized into **divisions by ordnance family**, each covering US and foreign items:
  Core → Demolition → Tools & Methods → Ground Ordnance → Air Ordnance → Improvised Explosive
  Devices → Underwater Ordnance (Navy) → Bio/Chem → Nuclear Ordnance. [S3]
  - Ground: landmines, grenades, projected munitions (projectiles, mortars, rockets).
  - Air: bombs, missiles, aircraft explosive hazards (egress systems, gun systems).
  - IED: improvised/terrorist devices.
  - Underwater: sea mines, torpedoes, search, foreign-materiel exploitation.
  - Nuclear: basic nuclear physics, radiation monitoring, decontamination.
- Pedagogical pattern worth copying: **a shared "Core" (electricity, explosives theory,
  publications, safety) and "Demolition" block first, then ordnance-family modules**.

### 1.2 US Army EOD (MOS 89D)
- Two-phase pipeline: **Phase 1 ≈7 weeks at the Ordnance School, Fort Lee (now Fort Gregg-Adams),
  Virginia**, then **Phase 2 ≈7 months at NAVSCOLEOD**. ~240 graduates/yr. [S4]
- Phase 1 scope (public descriptions): basic DC electronics, identification and hazards of US
  and foreign conventional munitions, EOD tool-kit care, introductory demolition, robotics
  familiarization, the Military Munitions Rule, soldier skills. [S4]
- The course moved from Redstone Arsenal to Fort Lee under BRAC 2005 (2011).

### 1.3 Civilian public-safety: FBI Hazardous Devices School (HDS), Redstone Arsenal, Alabama
- The **only** US facility that trains and certifies public-safety (police/fire) bomb
  technicians. Opened 1971; jointly run with the US Army until 2016 when the FBI took primary
  responsibility. 455-acre campus with mock town/village ranges. [S5, S6]
- Basic certification course is ~6 weeks (publicly described topics: electricity, fuzing,
  improvised explosives); **recertification every 3 years**. >20,000 responders certified. [S5]
- **NBSCAB (National Bomb Squad Commanders Advisory Board)**: 12 elected squad commanders from
  4 regions, all on record with HDS. Publishes the *National Guidelines for Bomb Technicians*,
  which define (a) bomb-technician certification requirements, (b) bomb-squad accreditation,
  (c) the profession's baseline doctrine and safety principles. Advises HDS on training.
  Some US states reference the Guidelines in statute (e.g., Nevada NRS 476.140). [S7]

### 1.4 ATF — National Center for Explosives Training and Research (NCETR), Redstone Arsenal
- Consolidates ATF explosives and fire programs. Public course lines: post-blast
  investigation, explosives specialist certification, advanced disposal techniques,
  homemade-explosives school. [S8]
- **Certified Explosives Specialist (CES)**: 2-year candidacy; ~11 weeks in-person training,
  **12 graduate semester hours in forensic science**, plus field casework. Notable because it
  explicitly blends operational and academic study. [S8]

## 2. United Kingdom

### 2.1 DEMS Training Regiment (Defence EOD, Munitions & Search)
- Part of the Royal School of Military Engineering Group. Formed 2009 by merging the Army School
  of Ammunition (founded 1922), the Defence EOD School and the National Search Centre; renamed
  2012. Sites: **MoD Kineton** (Warwickshire) and **MoD Bicester** (Oxfordshire). [S9, S10]
- Four training squadrons map neatly onto four domains: **Munitions** (ammunition theory and
  logistics), **Conventional Munitions Disposal**, **Specialist Search**, **IED Disposal**. [S10]
- Trains Army (RLC Ammunition Technicians/Officers, Royal Engineers EOD & Search), Royal Navy,
  RAF, government civilians and partner nations. [S10]

### 2.2 Ammunition Technician (AT) and Ammunition Technical Officer (ATO) — RLC
- UK model is distinctive: the bomb-disposal operator is first an **ammunition engineer**
  (through-life management: procurement, storage, inspection, repair, accident investigation)
  and only then an EOD/IEDD operator. [S11, S12]
- **AT basic course ~9 months**: a science phase at the Defence Academy/Royal Military College of
  Science (Shrivenham) — mathematics, ballistics, explosives and general chemistry, physics,
  metallurgy, electronics, weapons-system design — then a practical phase at DEMS. After ~3
  years' experience, a **Class 2 → Class 1 upgrade** (~3 months) adds incident investigation,
  large-scale demolitions and chemical/biological munitions disposal. [S12]
- **ATO**: commissioned officers; ~17–20-month technical course split between the Defence
  Academy (Shrivenham) and DEMS Kineton. Graduates command EOD troops or ammunition units. [S11]
- **Royal Engineers** own conventional (e.g., WWII air-dropped bomb) EOD and military search;
  RLC owns ammunition and IEDD. (Division of labour described in DEMS sources.) [S10]

## 3. NATO

### 3.1 NATO EOD Centre of Excellence (EOD COE), Trenčín, Slovakia
- Activated as a NATO COE 28 April 2011; framework nation Slovakia; sponsors include Czechia,
  Hungary, Poland, Romania, US. Works under Allied Command Transformation. [S13]
- Pillars: Education & Training, Lessons Learned, Concept & Doctrine Development,
  Experimentation. Courses include NATO EOD Doctrine, **EOD Staff Officer Training (SOT)**,
  CBRN incident management, **Homemade Explosives (basic/advanced)**. [S13, S14]
- SOT (2 weeks + distance-learning precursor): EO identification and hazards, EOD C2,
  NATO reporting/messages, threat analysis, planning EOR/EOD/IEDD — i.e., the *management and
  staff* layer, not bench skills. [S14]

### 3.2 AJP-3.18 (Ed. B, v1, Sept 2023) — Allied Joint Doctrine for EOD Support to Operations
- Ratified via **STANAG 2628**. Chapters: (1) Fundamentals of EOD, (2) EOD in the Joint
  Operating Environment, (3) Allied Joint EOD Command & Control, plus a Lexicon. [S15]
- Defines five **joint EOD capability subsets** — a useful top-level taxonomy for a course:
  1. Explosive Ordnance Reconnaissance (EOR)
  2. Explosive Ordnance Clearance
  3. Conventional Munitions Disposal (CMD)
  4. Improvised Explosive Device Disposal (IEDD)
  5. CBRN EOD (includes biological/chemical munition disposal) [S15]
- Operating domains: land, maritime (incl. underwater EOD and mine countermeasures), air
  (aircraft explosive hazards, airfield recovery), cyberspace. EOD contributes to Counter-IED
  mainly via "Defeat the Device" and technical exploitation ("Attack the Network"), and to
  electromagnetic-environment considerations. [S15]
- Note: several nations record reservations (they have no CBRN or underwater EOD capability) —
  showing these are specialist add-ons, not baseline.

## 4. UN / Humanitarian Mine Action and Ammunition Management

### 4.1 IMAS 09.30 (Explosive Ordnance Disposal) + T&EP 09.30/01/2022 (Competency Standards)
- IMAS are maintained by UNMAS with GICHD as custodian of the register. IMAS 09.30 covers safe
  EOD of mines, ERW, submunitions, (abandoned) chemical weapons; excludes nuclear/biological.
  Current amendment dated Sept 2022. [S16]
- **Four EOD levels** — the clearest public beginner→advanced ladder in the field: [S16, S17, S18]
  | Level | Conceptual scope (organizational description) | # competencies (T&EP 2022) |
  |---|---|---|
  | EOD 1 | Locate and destroy *specific items they were trained on*, in situ; team member | 93 |
  | EOD 2 | + assess transportability, multiple-item disposal; supervised by L3 | 89 |
  | EOD 3 | + full range of munitions (NEQ limit 50 kg), authorizes L2s; team leader | 156 |
  | EOD 3+ | Specialist modules (below) | 6 modules |
- **Seven competence categories** for Levels 1–3: Theory & Knowledge; Equipment Skills;
  Practical EOD Skills; Management & Leadership; Deployment & Post-Task; Reporting & Data;
  Storage & Transport. [S17]
- **Level 3+ modules**: Advanced Explosive Theory; Bulk Demolitions; Aerial Bombs; Guided
  Weapons; Chemical Ordnance (Basic); AFV (armoured vehicle) Clearance. [S17]
- Structure of the T&EP (useful as a data model for the course): Category → Competency Cluster
  (e.g., "Fuzes", "Explosive Theory", "Contamination Patterns") → Competency Root (e.g.,
  "Explosive Trains", "Artillery") → individual competency tagged with Level and assessment
  method (written exam, timed/assessed practical). Knowledge items deepen by level:
  *awareness (L1) → understand (L2) → describe/explain full cycle (L3)*. [S17]
- Lineage: evolved from **CEN Workshop Agreement CWA 15464:2005**; CEN transferred ownership to
  UNMAS/GICHD in 2011. [S19, S25]

### 4.2 T&EP 09.31/01 (IEDD Competency Standards) and UN IEDD Standards
- IEDD ladder for mine action: L1 Searcher/Deminer (recognition), L2 Team Assistant, L3 IEDD
  Operator (permissive environments), L3+ IEDD Advanced (threat-specific). Six categories:
  Theory & Knowledge (electrical systems, explosive theory, threat assessment, IED make-up),
  Equipment, Reporting & Data, Practical IEDD, Management & Leadership, Deployment. [S18, S21]
- **UN IEDD Standards (UNMAS/DPKO, May 2018)**: roles Basic Operator → Assistant → Intermediate
  Operator → Advanced Operator; threat assessment at national/area/scene levels; IEDD
  philosophy and principles; information management; risk education. [S22]

### 4.3 IATG (International Ammunition Technical Guidelines) — UNODA / UN SaferGuard
- 12 volumes on through-life conventional ammunition stockpile management; implementable at
  **Level 1 (basic) / 2 (intermediate) / 3 (advanced)** "risk-reduction process levels". [S23]
- **IATG 01.90 (3rd ed., March 2021) Personnel Competences**: competence model = behavioural +
  technical + performance; generic roles **Ammunition Handler, Processor, Accountant,
  Supervisor, Manager, Inspector (Regulator), Force Explosives Safety Officer**. Based on UK
  National Occupational Standards for Explosive Substances and Articles (EUExcert). Points to
  T&EP 09.30 for EOD-type activities (demilitarization, clearance after depot explosions). [S23]

### 4.4 GICHD and regional centres
- GICHD publishes field references (e.g., *Explosive Ordnance Guide for Ukraine*, 2022 —
  identification aid for qualified operators and survey/EOR staff). [S24]
- OSCE-supported ICExH (Tajikistan) delivers CWA-15464-aligned EOD Level 1–3 and Level 3+
  (demilitarization, IEDD, CBRNE) courses — example of a regional capacity-building centre. [S25]
- Journal of Conventional Weapons Destruction (JMU CISR) is the main open professional journal
  for the humanitarian sector. [S19, S18]

## 5. Police bomb squads elsewhere (brief)
- **Canada — Canadian Police College (RCMP), Ottawa**: Police Explosives Technician Course
  (PETC, 24 days + 8–10 h online pre-course, ≥80% pre-test): device identification, x-ray
  interpretation, equipment (robots, suits), search, forensic lab services, CBRN awareness.
  Revalidation course (PETVC) every 3–5 years. Ancillary: IED Recognition & Reconstruction,
  Post-Blast Scene Technician, Digital Radiography, Tactical PET. [S26, S27]
- **Israel — Israel Police Bomb Disposal Division**: ~1-year training; very high call volume
  (tens of thousands of suspicious-object calls/yr); operates Israel's Bomb Data Center. [S28]
- **Australia**: nationally recognized *Diploma of Police Bomb Technical Response* (Queensland
  Police first to align, 2021; 6-week course plus monthly/quarterly/annual sustainment);
  Australian Bomb Data Centre (AFP) for incident data. ADF EOD trains separately. [S29]

## 6. Professional organizations
- **IABTI** (founded 1974, HQ Fairfax VA, members from ~70 nations): in-service training,
  *The Detonator* magazine; **CIPBI** (Certified International Post Blast Investigator) —
  3 yrs experience, 200 training hours, 200-question exam, 5-year validity; domains: scene
  documentation, device reconstruction, evidence/lab analysis, expert testimony, wound
  dynamics. [S30, S31]
- **NATEODA** (National EOD Association, US, founded 1988): fraternal/heritage body for
  NAVSCOLEOD and HDS graduates; not a training provider. [S32]

## 7. Academic programs
- **Missouri S&T** — only US MS in Explosives Engineering (30 cr; choose 4 of 6 core:
  Principles of Explosives Engineering, Blasting Design, Demolition of Structures, Tunneling,
  Scientific Instrumentation for Explosives, Environmental Controls). Catalog also has
  detonation theory/thermochemistry, blast vibration and air-blast, pyrotechnics, and
  regulatory (ATF/DOT/EPA/OSHA) courses. [S33, S34]
- **New Mexico Tech / EMRTC** — Graduate Certificate in Explosives Engineering (12 cr, for
  BS mechanical engineers; explosives fundamentals + wave propagation); undergrad minor;
  EMRTC is a 40-sq-mile energetic-materials test range that also runs first-responder
  training. [S35, S36]
- **Cranfield University @ Defence Academy, Shrivenham** — MSc Explosives Ordnance Engineering
  (11 months FT; core: Intro to Explosives Engineering, Munitions & Target Response, Research
  Tools, Future Developments; electives incl. propellants, pyrotechnics, T&E of explosives,
  underwater munitions, C-IED capability, safety assurance, weapon life assessment). Short
  course "Introduction to Explosives Engineering" (5 days, MSc level: initiation, deflagration
  vs detonation, primary/secondary explosives, ordnance design from trigger to terminal
  effect). [S37, S38] Cranfield's former forensic-explosives MSc page now reports the
  programme as no longer available (Sept 2026).
- **Core textbooks used across these programmes**: Akhavan, *The Chemistry of Explosives*
  (RSC, 4th ed. 2022 — classification, combustion/deflagration/detonation, initiation,
  thermochemistry, kinetics, propellants/pyrotechnics) [S39]; Cooper, *Explosives Engineering*
  (Wiley — chemistry, burning, detonation, shock waves, initiation, scaling; assumes calculus
  physics/chemistry) [S40].

## 8. Synthesis

### 8.1 Common knowledge domains across all pathways
Every pathway (military, police, humanitarian, academic) converges on the same ~12 domains:

1. **Safety, risk & legal/regulatory framework** — explosives safety principles, safe
   distances concept, standards bodies (IMAS, IATG, NBSCAB, national law).
2. **Explosives science** — chemistry & classification (low/high, primary/secondary),
   deflagration vs detonation, thermochemistry, sensitivity/stability, ageing.
3. **Physics of effects** — blast waves and scaling, fragmentation, shaped-charge principle,
   thermal effects, ground/structure response.
4. **Electricity & electronics fundamentals** — DC circuits, power sources, switches/sensors
   (explicitly first in Army Phase 1, HDS, IEDD T&EP).
5. **Fuzing & explosive trains (theory)** — how munitions arm and function, conceptually; by
   far the largest single cluster in T&EP 09.30.
6. **Ordnance recognition & categorization** — by family: ground (mines, projectiles,
   mortars, rockets, grenades), air (bombs, missiles), underwater, submunitions; US/foreign.
7. **Improvised devices (IED) as a system** — component-level taxonomy, threat assessment,
   C-IED framework (defeat device / attack network / prepare force).
8. **Detection, search & diagnostics** — search doctrine, detector theory, imaging/x-ray
   interpretation, EOR.
9. **Tools, equipment & robotics** — remote platforms, PPE/bomb suit, protective principles
   (time–distance–shielding), EW environment awareness.
10. **Disposal methods (conceptual)** — the general families (destroy in situ, remove,
    low-order/deflagration, render safe) as *concepts*; the how-to stays operational.
11. **Specialist domains** — CBRN, underwater/maritime, aircraft hazards, nuclear, bulk
    demolitions, AFVs, guided weapons, HME.
12. **Command, reporting, information & forensics** — team roles, planning, reporting
    standards, data management, technical exploitation, post-blast investigation, expert
    evidence; plus ammunition management/storage/transport (UK/IATG emphasis).

### 8.2 Beginner / intermediate / advanced
| Tier | Anchor (IMAS/UN) | Typical content |
|---|---|---|
| Beginner | EOD L1 / IEDD L1 / IATG handler | Safety, recognition of trained items, basic explosive theory, team-member role, awareness-level fuzing |
| Intermediate | EOD L2 / IEDD L2 / AT Class 2 | Multi-item scenarios, transport/storage logic, "understand" level fuzing & explosive trains, supervised remote tasks |
| Advanced | EOD L3 / IEDD L3 / ATO / HDS-certified | Full range of munitions, "explain full cycle" theory, team leadership, planning, reporting, authorizing others |
| Specialist | EOD 3+ / IEDD 3+ / AT Class 1 / NATO specialist | Advanced explosive theory, aerial bombs, guided weapons, chemical, bulk demolitions, AFV, HME, CBRN, underwater, investigation |
| Staff / management | NATO SOT, ATO, bomb-squad commander, IATG manager/inspector | Doctrine, C2, threat analysis, capability planning, standards/accreditation |

### 8.3 Taught academically vs operationally
- **Academic (open, degree-level)**: explosives chemistry and thermochemistry, detonation and
  shock physics, blast/fragmentation effects and scaling, instrumentation & testing, materials,
  ordnance systems engineering (trigger → terminal effect), safety engineering, environmental
  and regulatory, forensic science/post-blast analysis, statistics of risk.
- **Operational (closed, institution-only)**: specific ordnance items' render-safe, tool
  techniques, IED countermeasures, live-explosive range work, tactical employment, national
  SOPs. Also practical assessment against the T&EP.
- **Hybrid** examples: UK AT/ATO (science phase at Shrivenham then DEMS), ATF CES (graduate
  forensic credits + field), Cranfield EOE MSc for serving officers.
- Implication for this course: everything in the "academic" list plus the *organizational*
  view of the operational list (what exists, why, how it is governed) is in scope.

### 8.4 Prerequisite relationships
```
Math (algebra→calculus) ─┬─> Physics (mechanics, waves) ──> Shock/detonation physics ──> Blast & fragmentation effects ──> Protection & standoff concepts
                         └─> Chemistry (thermo, kinetics) ─> Explosives chemistry & classification ─┘
Electricity & electronics ──> Initiation systems (concept) ──> Fuzing & explosive trains ──> Ordnance families ──> Specialist ordnance (3+)
                                                                              └──> IED as a system ──> C-IED framework
Safety & risk principles ──> (every practical domain)
Ordnance families + IED ──> Search/detection/diagnostics ──> Disposal-method concepts
Everything above ──> Command, planning, reporting ──> Forensics / post-blast / technical exploitation
Explosives science + ordnance ──> Ammunition management (storage, transport, compatibility, IATG)
```
Evidence: Army Phase 1 and HDS put electricity first; UK AT puts maths/physics/chemistry first;
Cooper's text assumes calculus physics/chemistry; T&EP knowledge items escalate from
awareness to full explanation across levels.

### 8.5 What a technician needs conceptually
1. A mental model of **energy release** (why some materials burn and others detonate; what
   makes something sensitive).
2. A model of **effects vs distance/shielding** (blast, fragments, heat) — the basis of all
   safety decisions.
3. A model of **how a munition is designed to function and stay safe** (safety/arming as a
   state machine) — highly analogous to software state machines, interlocks and fail-safes.
4. **Recognition and classification** skills across ordnance families.
5. **Systems thinking about improvised devices** (components, threat assessment, adversary
   adaptation) and the C-IED framework.
6. **Risk management and decision-making under uncertainty**; human factors.
7. **Standards, governance and documentation** (IMAS, IATG, AJP-3.18, NBSCAB) and reporting
   / data discipline.
8. **Investigation mindset** — evidence, reconstruction, lessons learned, exploitation.
