## How to work through this stage

1. Read 00.1 and do its classification exercises *before* opening the answers. The quality of your
   reasoning from the definitions is the skill, not the labels.
2. Play a Beginner Sim A scene once as an observer (00.1), then again as a systems engineer (00.2).
3. Spend most of 00.2's time on the data-model design exercise. It is the first place where your
   engineering background meets the field, and its event-sourced design recurs in Stages 7–9.
4. Take the [Stage 0 gate](assessments/stage-00.md).

## Where this stage leads

00.2 unlocks three branches of the [dependency graph](curriculum/course-outline.md): the physics path
([01.1](lessons/stage-01/lesson-01.md)), the chemistry path (02.1) and the recognition path (03.1),
as well as detection theory (05.1).

<div class="callout boundary">

**What this stage deliberately leaves out, and why.** Stage 0 describes organisations, standards,
roles and the incident life-cycle **at the level of decisions and information**. It does not
describe how any stage of an incident is carried out: no render-safe or disposal procedures, no
tool techniques, no cordon distances for specific items, no tactics, and no details of real devices.
The *act* stage of the life-cycle appears only as a choice among outcome families made on the basis
of risk, which is how it is analysed in the public doctrine. These skills are taught, under
supervision and with live equipment, only inside accredited institutions (HDS, NAVSCOLEOD, DEMS,
IMAS-accredited operators). Written instructions outside that setting would be unsafe and could be
misused. Everything the course *does* teach, including the physics, detection, robotics, decision
frameworks, data systems and forensic method, is what the engineering of this field actually needs.
